## Paper

Compound AI Systems.

## TLDR

Useful AI products are rarely a single model call. They are systems of retrievers, models, tools, policies, caches, and evaluators whose interactions determine production quality.

## Problem

Model-level benchmarks do not explain how a complete AI product behaves when retrieval, orchestration, tools, prompts, and fallback policies interact.

## Method

Treat the application as a graph of measurable components. Define system-level success criteria, isolate component failure modes, and run targeted evaluations alongside end-to-end tests.

## Key Insight

System quality is often improved more effectively by changing orchestration, data, or evaluation than by replacing the foundation model.

## Limitations

Component metrics can miss interaction effects. End-to-end judgments remain expensive, and the evaluation set must evolve as users discover new failure modes.

## Applications

- Which component owns each failure mode?
- What can be measured without an expensive human label?
- Where does complexity buy reliability—and where does it only hide uncertainty?
